The device

What SnapCath™ is

If a colleague forwarded you this page, start here. This is a plain description of the device, the kind you can repeat accurately to someone who has not seen it.

SnapCath™ is an external accessory for indwelling vascular access catheters. It is applied to the skin over the insertion site after the line is placed, and it is designed to handle two jobs in one device: holding the catheter in position, and covering the site.

It is a single unit with three parts:

  • An adhesive base that attaches to the skin around the insertion site.
  • A rigid shell that holds the catheter and sits over the site.
  • A hinged lid that opens and closes for site access.

Who it is being developed for: patients with a vascular access line that stays in for days or weeks, across inpatient, outpatient infusion, dialysis, long-term care, and home settings. Final indications for use will be established through the regulatory process, so nothing here should be read as a cleared indication.

Where it is: in development. There is no product to buy, no study to enroll in, and no timeline being promised. Lenneuro is a five-person company in Colville, Washington, in the 2026 MedTech Innovator cohort.

How this is generally handled now

A description of common practice, not a claim about any specific product.

  • Securement and site coverage are usually addressed by separate items: a securement or stabilization device, plus a transparent film dressing over the site.
  • Each is applied in its own step, and each has its own adhesive contacting the skin.
  • Dressings are changed on a set schedule, and sooner if they lift, become soiled, or get wet.
  • Inspecting or accessing the site generally means taking the dressing off and putting a new one on.
  • Keeping the site dry during bathing is managed separately, with covers or by avoiding water.

What SnapCath™ is designed to do

Design objectives under evaluation in development. Not tested performance claims.

  • Combine catheter securement and site coverage in one device, applied in a single step.
  • Limit how much adhesive contacts the skin during wear.
  • Allow the lid to open for routine site access without removing the adhesive base.
  • Provide water resistance for everyday wear.
  • Present the site through a design intended to support visual assessment.

The value case

How SnapCath™ will make your practice better

Two things tend to be true at once. What makes a device easier to live with on your unit is usually the same thing that makes the case for it at your institution. Here is where SnapCath™ lands on both, written so you can lift it straight into a conversation.

Your time

A site check stops being a procedure

A routine dressing change today is a multi-step sterile procedure, and in home and outpatient settings it can require a visit built around it. SnapCath™ is designed so the lid opens for routine site access without removing the adhesive base. Time per change is the line most institutions have the least visibility into and the most to gain from.

Patient outcomes

Three problems, one device

The things that hurt patients and budgets alike are downstream: central line associated bloodstream infection, unplanned line replacement after a dislodgement, and medical adhesive related skin injury. Each is a well documented category of avoidable harm and avoidable cost, and each is already tracked by most quality departments. SnapCath™ is being designed against all three at once rather than one at a time.

Continuity of care

The same device follows the patient

Inpatient, outpatient infusion, dialysis, long term care, and home are the same product and the same technique, which matters for in-servicing, for par levels, and for systems carrying post-acute risk. SnapCath™ is being designed to be simple enough for a patient or caregiver to manage at home, which is where the burden of the current bundle falls hardest.

Supply spend

One line item instead of a bundle

Securing and covering a catheter site today means stocking several separate items and applying them in sequence: a securement device, a transparent film dressing, an antimicrobial disc, plus the prep and tape around them. SnapCath™ is designed to do the anchoring and the covering in one device, which consolidates what materials management stocks, and what a review committee evaluates, into a single item.

Reimbursement

No new codes to secure

SnapCath™ is designed to fit inside existing line insertion and maintenance coding, and into home health and outpatient infusion bundles. There is no new code to obtain, no payer negotiation to open, and no separate reimbursement pathway for finance to build before the product can be used.

Regulatory status

Class I, 510(k) exempt

SnapCath™ is classified as a Class I, 510(k) exempt device, so no FDA submission is required at launch. Lenneuro has completed its exemption documentation and established an eQMS. For a review committee, that removes one of the more common sources of timeline uncertainty when evaluating a device from an early stage company.

Everything above describes design objectives under evaluation during development. SnapCath™ has not been cleared or approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, and none of this should be read as demonstrated performance, a cleared indication, or a claim of clinical or economic benefit.
